# Financial Derivatives and Risk Management

A derivative changes a pattern of cash flows. Whether that change improves a decision depends on the original exposure, the trading assumptions, and the risks that remain. This independent study edition develops those connections through contracts, replication, numerical checks, and risk measurement.

The source course is **NPTEL Financial Derivatives & Risk Management, IIT Roorkee**, taught by **Prof. J. P. Singh**. The supplied playlist contains a short introduction and sixty teaching recordings. The book groups them into twenty-four chapters. It preserves the source identities and both playlist and lecture order, including put–call parity, whose upload appears at the end of the supplied playlist.

## How to read this edition

The intended reader is mathematically mature and new to derivatives. Algebra, linear algebra, elementary calculus, and probability are useful prerequisites. Finance-specific terms are introduced before use. Brownian motion, Itô calculus, pricing probabilities, and risk measures are built when the argument needs them, rather than invoked as unexplained vocabulary.

Each chapter begins with a consequential problem, develops its model under explicit assumptions, and returns to the original decision. Worked calculations and counterexamples test the mechanism. Six exercises with full solutions and an executable Python laboratory accompany each chapter. A lab checks its finite examples and numerical algorithms; it is not formal proof of every statement in the chapter.

The source recordings determine the coverage spine. Definitions, derivations, examples, primary-source extensions, code, and exercise solutions are independently developed teaching material. A timestamp locates a related source discussion; it does not attribute every argument in that section to the lecturer. [References and evidence limits](references.html) explain that boundary.

## Three threads to keep separate

- **Cash flows and replication:** identify who pays what, when, and in which currency. Exact statewise cancellation supports stronger conclusions than a favorable average outcome.
- **Models and probability:** distinguish physical scenarios from pricing probabilities, and mathematical assumptions from observations about actual markets.
- **Risk and implementation:** test residual exposure, finite hedge intervals, numerical convergence, funding needs, and losses beyond the modeled quantile.

The reader works offline after downloading the repository. Mathematics, navigation, code-copy controls, and local interactive experiments use bundled assets. Opening recordings, papers, and official product documentation requires internet access. Use the chapter's editable Markdown source for annotation, or Print / save PDF for a reading copy.
